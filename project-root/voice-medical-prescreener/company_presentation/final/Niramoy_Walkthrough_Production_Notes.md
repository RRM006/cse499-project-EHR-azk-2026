## How it was captured

The project was run in the recording environment exactly as it runs on your machine —
`uvicorn backend.app.main:app`, SQLite, all three portals — and driven with a scripted browser at
1600×900. Each scene is one continuous take; the edit only trims, cross-dissolves and, where a form
is being filled in, speeds the picture up.

**Live during the recording**

* Patient sign-in, the real OTP (issued by the server, read from its own log, typed in), the
  voice-first conversation screen, the language toggle, the portal's own typing fallback, and every
  answer stored through the normal pipeline.
* The patient's raw-transcript document — generated by `POST /visits/{uuid}/documents/transcript`
  during the shoot, then displayed unaltered.
* The whole medic workflow: the triage queue, the verbatim panel, the ten extracted fields, the risk
  panel, vitals recorded into `patients`, and the referral that really moved the case to
  `awaiting_doctor`.
* The whole doctor workflow: the safety panel, the prescription (diagnosis typed by hand),
  `Accept & Write to EHR`, and the **HL7 FHIR R4 document bundle the app wrote at that moment** —
  the JSON on screen at 2:35 is that file, opened straight from disk.

**Not live, and why**

The AI-provider hosts (Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter, Cerebras) are blocked by the recording sandbox's
egress policy, so the three steps that call a model could not run during the shoot:
M3 extraction, M7 follow-up generation, and the model half of M10/M11.

Those results were therefore **not re-created** — the case shown is your own genuine record
(visit 20), extracted, questioned and assessed by the real pipeline when the providers *were*
reachable, and rendered here by the real portals. The rule-based red-flag check is local and did run
live: it matched `chest pain` in the patient's own recorded words and forced the tier to Critical.

**The microphone.** Browser speech recognition needs a live mic and Google's speech service, neither
of which exists in the capture environment. The recording therefore uses the portal's own shipped
typing fallback, chosen on camera through the `[🎤 Speak] [⌨ Type]` switch, and the narration says so
plainly at 0:44. Typed and spoken answers go through the *same* endpoint and differ only in
`source` (`manual` vs `mic`), so nothing downstream is affected. No frame implies that typing was
microphone input; the voice-first behaviour is shown through the portal's own live state —
"Please wait — the microphone will start by itself" — which is genuine.

## Demo-data preparation

`prepare_demo_case.py` (in the project root) does two things and nothing else:

1. **Privacy.** Every patient's phone number and display name is replaced with an unmistakable demo
   value. No clinical content is touched — transcripts, extracted fields, risk assessments, XAI
   reasons and documents are exactly as the application wrote them.
2. **Workflow reset.** The case is put back to `awaiting_review` with no assigned doctor so the
   medic → doctor → EHR hand-off could be performed live, and its visit timestamps are moved to
   today so the queue's genuine wait-time column reads `42m` instead of `18d 6h`. Not one clinical
   value moves.

Nothing on screen is a real patient. No API key, `.env`, password, prompt or source file appears in
any frame.

## Voice and captions

The voice-over is **Kokoro**, a neural TTS run locally and offline in the recording environment
(voice `am_michael`, 24 kHz, 153 wpm). No cloud TTS was reachable, and none was used.

Captions are burned in and also delivered as `Niramoy_Walkthrough_Final.srt` (54 cues, ≤ 56
characters per line, no overlaps). They are the narration **verbatim** — the only differences are
that acronyms spelled out for the synthesiser ("H L seven", "A I", "P D F") are written properly
(HL7, AI, PDF). `python narration.py` checks this word for word and prints
"captions match the narration word for word".

---

## Claims deliberately not made

No accuracy, word-error-rate, latency or precision/recall figure appears anywhere — the project has
no formal evidence for one yet, so the film claims none. Nothing implies autonomous diagnosis or
emergency escalation: the narration says the system narrows and the clinician decides, which is what
the code does.
